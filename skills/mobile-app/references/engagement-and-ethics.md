# Engagement and Ethics

Use this reference for retention, notifications, habits, progress, gamification, rewards, social mechanics, sharing, and referrals.

## Contents

1. Define healthy retention
2. Choose mechanics by user benefit
3. Design progress and streaks
4. Design rewards and ceremony

## 1. Define healthy retention

Define retention as repeated receipt of value, not inability to leave.

Write:

```text
Beneficial repeat outcome:
Natural need cadence:
User-chosen cue:
Evidence of growing competence or saved effort:
Healthy finish or pause state:
What the user keeps if they leave:
```

Do not create an artificial daily habit for a weekly or episodic need. Let utility tasks end quickly. Let experiential use continue only through informed, voluntary choice.

Prefer retention from:

- better results over time;
- saved work and reduced future effort;
- visible mastery or real progress;
- reliable continuity across devices and interruptions;
- genuine community and collaboration;
- user-controlled personalization that can be inspected and exported.

Reject retention from anxiety, threatened loss, sunk cost, obscured exit, or deliberate non-portability.

## 2. Choose mechanics by user benefit

Choose no mechanic until the underlying behavior works without one.

| Need | Consider | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand progress | Finite progress, milestones, history | Represent real completion or competence |
| Build a chosen routine | Flexible plan, reminders, recovery | Match natural cadence; allow pause and repair |
| Improve skill | Personal bests, feedback, levels tied to mastery | Do not reward attendance alone |
| Feel supported | Encouragement and private or small-group acknowledgement | Recognize effort without requiring rank; keep audience controlled |
| Compare performance | Personal baseline or opt-in matched cohort | Make comparison fair, winnable, and non-shaming |
| Mark a meaningful result | Proportional motion, haptic, summary | Do not delay access or celebrate harmful activity |
| Add delight | Bounded cosmetic surprise | Never combine opaque chance with payment or material value |

Add one mechanic at a time. Remove it if users spend more effort managing the game layer than performing the beneficial activity.

Use completion drive only for finite, user-chosen goals. Show how much remains and what completion means. Never keep a task artificially unfinished to provoke return.

## 3. Design progress and streaks

Make progress:

- truthful, legible, and tied to an outcome;
- based on a user-selected goal or a clearly justified default;
- durable across missed days;
- private by default;
- correctable when data is wrong;
- accessible without relying on color, animation, or shape alone.

Prefer accumulated evidence such as lessons mastered, workouts completed, money saved, projects finished, or recovery consistency.

If a streak genuinely fits:

- let the user choose cadence and reminder;
- define the minimum continuity action separately from the beneficial goal, dose, quality, or mastery target;
- support rest days, pause, grace, repair, and schedule changes;
- preserve historical achievement after a miss;
- represent completed activity, grace, rest, and missed activity truthfully;
- do not use guilt, sad characters, public loss, or alarming language;
- do not sell relief from anxiety the mechanic created;
- let the user hide or disable it;
- measure recovery after a missed period, not only longest streak.

Do not lower the qualifying action merely to improve streak participation without measuring whether beneficial activity declines. Track continuity, goal completion, dose, quality, and mastery separately.

Avoid streaks for minors, mental health vulnerability, financial trading, gambling-like products, or behaviors where overuse can cause harm unless a qualified safety review establishes a clear benefit.

## 4. Design rewards and ceremony

Use anticipation, reveal, and celebration only to give appropriate weight to a meaningful user result:

1. Signal that a real result is ready.
2. Reveal it clearly with proportional feedback.
3. Provide an afterglow: summary, reflection, next choice, or optional share artifact.

Provide immediate access, skip, mute, reduced-motion, and non-haptic alternatives where appropriate.

Celebrate:

- completed beneficial effort;
- genuine mastery or recovery;
- collaboration and community contribution;
- a result the user wanted.

Do not celebrate:

- risky trades, spending, betting, or excessive use;
- a purchase primarily because it makes the platform money;
- fabricated milestones;
- random rewards purchased with money;
- coercive invitations or data disclosure.

In-product surprise must be bounded, cosmetic, free, and inconsequential. Do not sell randomized rewards. If a separate, legally permitted free promotion uses chance to award material value, disclose rules and odds close to entry and obtain qualified review; in high-risk contexts, do not use chance at all.

## 5. Design social systems

Make social participation opt-in and audience-controlled. Default sensitive activity to private.

Before adding a leaderboard or social feed, specify:

- what healthy behavior becomes visible;
- who can see it;
- whether cohorts are genuinely comparable;
- how new or less able users can succeed;
- how users hide, block, report, leave, and delete;
- how abuse, fraud, cheating, and unwanted contact are handled;
- what happens when the system is wrong.

Prefer personal baselines, cooperative goals, small relevant cohorts, and lightweight acknowledgement over one global ranking.

Use acknowledgement without rank when recognition or support is the actual need. If comparison is valuable, require explicit opt-in, explain cohort membership, account for ability and opportunity, and provide an easy return to a personal baseline. Never turn completion into a surprise message that the user is “falling behind.”

Do not turn health, wealth, attractiveness, popularity, or productivity into public status by default. Do not make quitting a public failure.

Make share cards use authentic user data with explicit preview and editing. Distinguish observation from inference, include the source timeframe, and state material uncertainty or limitation. Let users edit, hide, or regenerate the artifact. Exclude sensitive traits and private fields by default; do not issue a diagnostic or identity verdict. Provide accessible alternative text and let users choose audience and destination. Never gate core value behind sharing, contacts access, reviews, or referrals.

When sharing uses a link, scope access to the intended recipient or audience where feasible. For private content, provide expiration and revocation, prevent search indexing and revealing link previews, and make deletion remove future access. Explain whether recipients can reshare, copy, or download.

## 6. Design notifications and return cues

Earn notification permission in context. State the concrete user benefit and show the categories they can control.

Every notification must be:

- expected from a user action, chosen plan, or material event;
- timely and useful at receipt;
- truthful about urgency and system state;
- deep-linked to the relevant, recoverable action;
- suppressible by category and globally;
- respectful of timezone, quiet hours, accessibility, and sensitive lock screens.

Use the least interruptive channel that works: in-app status before badge, badge before push, push before time-sensitive alert.

Avoid generic “we miss you,” fake activity, social pressure, expiring progress, or repeated prompts after refusal. Do not ask for push permission at launch unless the app cannot deliver its core promised value without it and that need is obvious.

Measure helpful action after receipt, disable/mute rate, uninstall, complaint, interruption regret, and whether the same outcome would occur without the notification.

## 7. Detect dark patterns

Run this test:

> Would the design still work if the user fully understood the mechanism, price, data use, odds, and easiest exit?

Block:

- **Obstruction:** buried close, cancellation, export, deletion, refund, or downgrade.
- **Misdirection:** weak visual treatment for the safer or cheaper option.
- **Confirmshaming:** “No, I prefer failure” or “I’ll risk it.”
- **Sneaking:** late fees, add-ons, renewal, contacts upload, or secondary data use.
- **False urgency/scarcity:** resetting timers, fictional inventory, or routine “one-time” offers.
- **Forced action:** sharing, review, invitation, permissions, or account creation before necessary value.
- **Interface interference:** disguised controls, preselected consent, trick toggles, or inconsistent button meaning.
- **Social coercion:** public streak loss, status demotion, parasocial pressure, or identity lock-in.
- **Fabrication:** fake progress, loaders, work, personalization, scores, testimonials, presence, or scientific authority.
- **Infinite extraction:** no finish state, deliberate progress erasure, or endless open loops intended to prevent stopping.

Replace coercion with clear value, transparent tradeoffs, good defaults, reminders chosen by the user, risk reduction, and easy recovery.

## 8. Measure benefit and harm

Pair every engagement metric with outcome and agency:

| Product signal | Pair with |
| --- | --- |
| Return rate | Beneficial repeat outcome |
| Streak continuation | Goal completion, beneficial dose/quality, stress, pause use, recovery after miss |
| Notification opens | Helpful task completion, mute/uninstall |
| Time in app | Outcome quality and intended-session completion |
| Social sharing | Voluntary share, accidental disclosure, complaints |
| Competition participation | Cohort fairness, cheating, dropout |
| Reward claims | Regret, excessive use, spend, comprehension |

Track, where appropriate:

- sessions extending beyond user intent;
- regretted or overnight use;
- compulsive checking;
- notification disablement;
- recovery after interruption;
- pressure, shame, or “felt misled” responses;
- refund, chargeback, complaint, and support themes;
- successful pause, export, cancellation, reset, and deletion.

When testing streak framing, use a matched non-streak reminder comparator—not only silence—and hold channel, cadence, timing, personalization, reinforcement, and surrounding copy constant. Other active treatments compare complete intervention packages but do not isolate the streak frame. Report exposure, participation, missing outcome data, beneficial real-world outcome, and harm. Platform use alone is insufficient.

Stop or roll back when business lift depends on lower comprehension or agency, or when user benefit does not improve with retention.
